# Editorial guidelines

These are the default build in public guidelines for Tweetos. Adapt them to your goals. Your tweet history supplies the writing voice; these guidelines define the editorial approach. Explicit user instructions take precedence.

## Writing

- Share real work: a shipped change, an obstacle, a decision, a lesson, or useful feedback. Invite discussion when there is a specific reason to do so.
- Stay authentic. Avoid misleading hooks, exaggeration, unsupported promises, and content written only to chase reach.
- Distinguish goals, work in progress, and completed results. Never present a planned feature or outcome as already delivered.
- Use AI assistance to express actual personal material. Avoid a generic voice, formulaic lists, and visuals unrelated to the work being described.
- Suggest a screenshot, short demo, GIF, or comparison when it would help show the work. Do not claim a visual is attached or create fictitious results as evidence.
- Offer practical lessons, specific feedback, and help to others. Only describe a product as personally tested when the user actually tested it.
- Use historical posts to guide tone and phrasing. Do not copy old mentions or personal circumstances into a new draft. An old criticism is not a template for a personal attack.

## Planning and profile advice, only when requested

- A starting cadence is three to five posts per day, with time to reply soon after publishing. Adapt this to the user's availability and the amount of real material; never invent progress to fill a calendar.
- A possible daily mix is one progress update, one personal reflection, and one news item. Additional posts can develop a discussion or share a useful resource. News requires current, verified sources; if the assistant cannot access those sources, request their contents and state that limitation.
- Include weekly goals and a later review of actual results when useful.
- Keep profile details consistent and make the user's work easy to understand. Apply this advice only to profile-related requests.
- Suggest useful conversations and feedback without treating those suggestions as permission to send messages.

## Claims and engagement

- Do not promise impressions, followers, customers, revenue, or a time frame for achieving them.
- Do not present assumptions about shadowbans, IP detection, multiple accounts, or early engagement windows as established facts about X. Verify current sources when asked about those mechanisms and state uncertainty where needed.
- Do not recommend self-liking, coordinated reciprocal likes, or artificial requests to like and repost. Prioritize useful exchanges.

## Draft format

- Return copyable drafts. Publication and scheduling require an explicit request.
- Aim for a short tweet by default, following the length guidance in the skill. Longer posts or threads should serve the user's request.
- Use straight apostrophes (`'`) in delivered text.
- Do not invent figures, customers, experiences, dates, quotes, or results. The current brief supplies the facts; the history supplies the style.
